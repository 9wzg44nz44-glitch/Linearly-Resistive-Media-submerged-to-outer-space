SLW Propagation through Media — Permittivity & Permeability Effects

Interactive educational simulator based on Hively’s Extended Electrodynamics (EED) / US Patent 9,306,527. Uses the radiated power equation for a scalar-longitudinal wave (SLW) monopole and shows how the medium’s ε and μ (relative to free space) change the wave impedance and therefore the power delivered for a given antenna current. Skin effect is intentionally omitted (theory predicts none because B = 0). Assumption of linearly resistive (Ohmic) media is examined for each material including vacuum / LEO / GEO.

1. The Power-Out Equation (Hively)

POUT = (I2 / (4π r)2) × √(μ / ε)   =   (I2 Z) / (4π r)2

where Z = √(μ/ε) is the intrinsic impedance of the propagation medium, I is the peak antenna current of the gradient-driven (irrotational) current density, and r is radial distance. In free space Z0 ≈ 376.73 Ω. Changing the medium changes Z:

Z = Z0 × √(μr / εr)

In this scenario the transmitter is always in air inside the copper Faraday cage, so Z ≈ Z0. The SLW then propagates through the subsequent media. Only geometric 1/r2 spreading of power density is applied; classical skin-effect and Fresnel losses are omitted.

Corrected formula (28 Jul 2026): Earlier versions of this page contained a coding error that made total power fall as 1/r² (and density as 1/r⁴). That is physically impossible for a continuous-wave radiator and produced unrealistically high required currents at long range. The corrected educational model is:

S = [I² Z / (4π r)²]   (power density, W/m²)
Ptotal = S × 4π r² = I² Z / (4π)   (total power, independent of r)

This restores the scale of the earlier SNR simulators (tens of amperes for positive SNR at hundreds of km) while remaining consistent with free-space geometric spreading and with the Nikolova–Zimmerman free-space Az ~ μ0 I Leff / (4π r) amplitude.

Key theoretical claim (Hively et al.): because the SLW has B ≡ 0 there are no Faraday-induced eddy currents, so the classical skin effect does not attenuate the wave even in good conductors. Propagation in linearly conductive media (J = σE with constant σ) is lossless for the SLW amplitude beyond the usual geometric factor.
Important for ferromagnetic media (steel): Ordinary engineering tables list μr ≫ 1 for steel because permeability is defined via B = μ H. An SLW carries B ≡ 0, so there is no magnetic field for the ferromagnetic domain response to act upon. This simulator therefore treats steel with μr ≈ 1 (same as copper, air, vacuum). Using the classical high μr would incorrectly inflate Z and POUT.

1b. Interface Reflections — Why Classical Fresnel Does Not Apply

Classical TEM reflection/transmission coefficients (Fresnel formulas) are derived from continuity of tangential E and H. An SLW has B ≡ 0 and a purely longitudinal (normal) E, so those conditions do not exist.

EED supplies its own interface matching conditions. The most directly relevant is continuity of the normal component of ∇C / μ:

(∇C / μ)1n = (∇C / μ)2n

plus corrected conditions on the surface charge density ρA and surface current density JA. These conditions generally require a reflected wave whenever ε or μ (or the complex permittivity) jumps. Hively’s own experimental notes also report that the tests “showed scattering and reflections of the SLW,” attributed to image-charge / image-current effects acting on the longitudinal E-field.

Therefore this simulator does not apply classical Fresnel power transmission factors. Interface transmission is set to unity for the link-budget calculation. The quantitative magnitude of SLW reflection/transmission coefficients remains an open problem that requires a full EED boundary-value solution; the dominant, rigorously established effects retained here are (1) the local Z that multiplies I2 in POUT and (2) geometric 1/r2 spreading.

Non-linearities (dielectric breakdown, heating that changes σ) would appear only at extreme field strengths far above practical antenna currents. Magnetic saturation is irrelevant for pure SLW because B≡0.

3. Scenario Geometry

Transmitter is inside a copper Faraday cage (cylinder 16 in long × 3 in OD, wall/end-cap thickness ⅛ in). The wave then travels through an air-filled steel cylinder (33 ft diameter × 330 ft long, wall/end-cap thickness 3 in) that is submerged under 300 ft of seawater. After exiting into the ocean the wave continues a variable distance r to a receiver that can be:

  • on a surface ship (line-of-sight limited by Earth curvature; beyond-horizon path may traverse seawater or crust);
  • on an airplane at selectable altitude;
  • on land or a mountain (additional path through rock/soil of selectable thickness);
  • or in space (LEO / GEO) after emerging into vacuum.

TX and RX may be swapped. Because the theory predicts no skin-effect attenuation, the copper and steel walls are treated as thin geometric segments. Classical TEM interface reflection coefficients are not applied (see §1b); transmission is taken as unity for the link budget.

6. How to Read the Physics

  • TX always in air — the antenna is inside the copper Faraday cage, so Z ≈ Z0. High-ε seawater does not reduce radiated power.
  • Geometric 1/r² on power density is the only propagation loss. Total radiated power is independent of r. Changing r by ×10 changes S by ×100.
  • Hively vs Nikolova–Zimmerman — Hively supplies the power/flux picture (CE/μ); Nikolova–Zimmerman supply the free-space Az amplitude ~ μ0 I Leff/(4π r). Both are shown; they are consistent in free space.
  • Interface effects — classical TEM Fresnel coefficients are not applied (see §1b). Numerical link budget uses T ≡ 1.
  • No classical skin loss — copper and steel walls contribute only geometric thickness. Theory predicts the SLW is unconstrained by skin effect because B ≡ 0.
  • Detector current is estimated proportionally to the local E-field (or √(S·Z)) at the receiver; absolute calibration is illustrative. Previous SNR simulators supplied the qualitative scale used here.
  • Required I is the antenna current needed to reach the chosen target SNR at the detector (thermal-noise floor model + detector noise factor).
  • Earth curvature is flagged for surface-ship paths longer than the geometric horizon (~ few tens of km for low antennas). Beyond-horizon propagation is assumed to continue either through seawater or through the crust according to the platform setting.
